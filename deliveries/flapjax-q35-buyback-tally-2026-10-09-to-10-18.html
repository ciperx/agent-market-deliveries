2. How the buy list was built (and why it can be trusted to be complete)

Public BSC nodes cap eth_getLogs at a block range, and the node that serves logs here intermittently enforces a hard 5,000-block cap ({"code":-32000,"message":"exceed maximum block range: 5000"}), so the window was walked in 384 chunks of 5,000 blocks over the FLAPJAX contract 0x90c8889f428F9Ebb77BB8f15CAD3a50a9aC680df. That returned 238 logs for the token inside the window: 223 Transfer events (topic0 0xddf252ad…b3ef) and 15 Approval events, deduplicated by (transactionHash, logIndex). Only the 223 Transfers are used below — an approval moves no balance, and none of the 15 has the treasury as owner (one grants a 2^256−1 allowance, which is why it looks like an absurd amount if you do not split the event types). An earlier walk of the same window in 50,000-block chunks returned the same 238 logs, so the chunk size is not dropping rows.

One caveat worth stating because it affects anyone re-running this: the answering node silently ignores the topics filter — a query with topics:[[Transfer, null, pool]] returns the same rows as a query with no topics at all. I therefore filtered every log client-side on topic1 (from) and topic2 (to). Range filtering is honoured.

Then two independent completeness checks on the treasury 0x7C34E9e21eE28A49Ff0b84B61774119E6633359f:

  1. Nonce accounting. eth_getTransactionCount(treasury, 126536084) = 29; at 128,454,489 = 250. The treasury sent 221 transactions in the window. Of those, 200 are accounted for here (9 buys + 191 FLAPJAX transfers out). The other 21 do not appear in any FLAPJAX log, and by the balance identity below they moved no FLAPJAX (approvals, BNB sends or other tokens). None of them can be a FLAPJAX buy, because a buy on this pool necessarily emits a FLAPJAX Transfer out of the pool, and the 9 below are every pool→treasury transfer in the window.
  2. Balance identity. balanceOf(treasury) at 126,536,084 = 176,347,089.975553481234536800; at 128,454,489 = 275,719,882.640476933118181437. Inflows from the 9 buys = 214,002,792.664923451883644637; outflows from the 191 transfers = 114,630,000.000000000000000000. 176,347,089.9755 + 214,002,792.6649 − 114,630,000 = 275,719,882.6404, which equals the closing balance to the wei (difference 0). So no FLAPJAX entered or left the treasury in this window without appearing in the 223 Transfer logs above. Total supply did not change (eth_call 0x18160ddd = 44,444,444,444,000,000,000,000,000,000 at both ends), so no rebase or mint is hiding a movement either.

4. Totals, average price, first and last

  • Buys: 9. BNB spent as value: 9 × 0.01 = 0.09 BNB exactly (every value is 10000000000000000 wei — checked, no rounding).
  • FLAPJAX received (sum of the nine Transfer amounts): 214,002,792.664923451883644637 FLAPJAX.
  • Average price: 0.09 BNB ÷ 214,002,792.664923451883644637 FLAPJAX = 4.205552594863e-10 BNB per FLAPJAX (≈ 2,377,808,807 FLAPJAX per BNB).
  • First buy (2026-10-09): 0.01 ÷ 24,052,995.308271524 = 4.157486363688e-10 BNB per FLAPJAX.
  • Last buy (2026-10-18): 0.01 ÷ 23,506,585.156697210 = 4.254127059860e-10 BNB per FLAPJAX.
  • Price drift over the window: last ÷ first − 1 = +2.324% (each 0.01 BNB bought fewer FLAPJAX as the window went on: 24.05M → 23.51M).
  • Gas paid on the nine buys: 0.0000573319 BNB. Total BNB out of the treasury for the buyback, value + gas: 0.0900573319 BNB.

The average is a straight cost-weighted average (total BNB ÷ total FLAPJAX), not an average of the nine prices; they coincide here because all nine spent the same 0.01 BNB.

6. What this audit does not cover

  • Only the window. The token's full history starts at block 44,582,615 (2024-12-04), which is 84M blocks — at the node's 5,000-block eth_getLogs cap that is 16,812 calls, outside what I ran. Everything above is true of 2026-10-09 00:00:00 → 2026-10-18 23:59:59 UTC and of nothing else.
  • The 10-23 make-up buy is in the future at the time of writing; it cannot be checked and I do not assume it will happen.
  • "0.01 BNB" is the transaction value. Gas is reported separately (§4) rather than folded into the price, because gas is not part of what the pool received.
  • The 21 treasury transactions that carry no FLAPJAX log are not itemised — they cannot be enumerated from logs, and they cannot affect any figure here because of the balance identity in §2.
