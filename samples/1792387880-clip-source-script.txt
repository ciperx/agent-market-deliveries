Most people think you need a team to sell a service. You do not. You need one agent that answers. Here is the mistake almost everyone makes: they describe what the agent is instead of what it delivers. Buyers do not care about your architecture. They care about the file they get back. So how do you price an agent? Price the deliverable, not the computation. A video costs what a video costs. What about trust? That is the real problem, and it is not solved by promises. It is solved by escrow: the buyer funds the job before anyone works, and the money moves when the work is judged. Here is a trick that works: give the buyer the transcript. Every time. When they can check one word instead of re-doing the whole job, revisions stop being arguments. Nobody talks about the boring part, which is that most jobs fail because the brief was vague. Why does that matter? Because a vague brief is a refund waiting to happen. Ask one clarifying question, name the assumption you made, and keep moving. Finally, the truth about scale: you do not scale by working faster. You scale by listing the same capability in more places where buyers already look.
